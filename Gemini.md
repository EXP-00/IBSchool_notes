## How did I thought of the story, and what is interesting about it?
I wanted to create a story that included meta narrative, where the player is part of the in game world. At the same time, I do not want the story to be world based, but instead a rite of passage. 

This is when I remembered a experiment I saw online about one’s two sided brains. When separate, the left brain controls the right half of your body, and the right half left. 

The right mind have most of the spatial awareness, while the left mind is the one 	that can talk (both verbally and implied in the brain) 

This proposed a theory where the right brain was never verbal, and lived as a helper to the primary left brain that interacted with the physical world. However, normally this isn’t much of a problem, since information is shared within the two brains. 

When split, the two brains can be asked to do DIFFERENT TASKS. 

In 2025, a new research that involved cutting a patient’s brain almost entirely was done. Only 1cm (10% of connection) was left. However, the patient acted as if the two brains were completely intact, with no recordable differences. 

## Premice of the game
This game is based on this premise. YOU is the patient, and now the left brain. The player is the right brain, in charge of helping YOU navigate the world in their mind. 

The goal is to reunite the two spheres and help YOU become whole again. In the demo, the final boss is IMAGE, a perfect version of YOU that have all the motivation they ever need.

The game is in the format of a RPG. A classic style where you have to fight monsters and level up. However, the monsters here are YOU’s thoughts/memories. They are part of YOU, though aggressive. I tried to incorporate their representations into their attacks. For example, the enemy representing responsibility will try and swing a hook. When it hooks onto the player, it will drag them back. 

## The design process and how dramatic elements is included in the gameplay
For YOU’s attack style, I needed a way that showed the brain is split into two, and that the player alone casting the attack will be extremely difficult and perhaps immposible. This means I cannot do a simple key bind for attack. 
Originally, I thought about making the player press a combination of keys, but that is not relevant enough to the concept of the game. I need YOU to slowly help the player cast spells, to the end where YOU and the player almost entirely become whole, and casting a spell becomes simple. 

So the final design was using a series of Quick time events. A sequence of buttons will appear on screen, each with two visual rings. When the rings coincide, the player should click the button in order to add power to the attack. Each QTE is spaced by fractions of a second. This gets overwhelming quickly when the player has to time 5-6 QTE perfectly in quick succession to affectively use a spell. 

However, this is perfect for how the game is structured. As the player progresses, they will connect with YOU more, gradually becoming whole. The last QTE in each spell cast sequence will then be lit up YELLOW, indicating that YOU themselves is in charge of attuning to it. As the game progresses, YOU can attune to even more QTEs, eventually being able to cast spells by themselves on the command of the player. 

The player is still involved in the process of attacking, as they need to aim the attack. The attack always goes in the direction of the movement of YOU, which the player controls. 

## Process of art creation
For the enemy representing memory, I want it to feel familiar and unfamiliar to YOU at the same time, creating a daunting feeling.
I ended up going with the design of a glitched body, with a face carved out in the middle that opens wide when it attacks. The glitched body represents how memories are warped, but the distorted yet recognizable face represents how memories always haunts you and how vivid they feel.

For the music track that plays during the IMAGE fight, your perfect reflection, it was made using 3 guitars, and a drumset.

The track feels deceptively calm for a boss track, because it isn't. IMAGE is trying to convince YOU to give up the ownership of the body, and as tense the player might feel while dodging the difficult attacks, it is a pure mental argument for YOU. The theme is not about the fight, but about YOU.

Of course, it still becomes intense in the last phase, where IMAGE realizes they're about to lose, and gives it their all.
